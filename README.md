# Online Retail II Analysis

## Project Goal

Turn two years of raw transactions from a UK online giftware wholesaler into answers a business owner can act on:

- **Revenue:** how is it developing, and how seasonal is it?
- **Concentration:** which countries and products drive it?
- **Customers:** how well does the business keep them, who are its most valuable ones, and what is a customer worth?

The deliverables are reproducible notebooks, a documented cleaning log, charts, and a Power BI dashboard.

## Data

- **Source:** [Online Retail II](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/502/online+retail+ii), UCI Machine Learning Repository.
- **Content:** All transactions of a UK-based, registered non-store online retailer (mainly giftware, many wholesale customers) between 01/12/2009 and 09/12/2011.
- **File:** `data/raw/online_retail_II.xlsx`, two sheets: `Year 2009-2010` and `Year 2010-2011`. The raw file is never modified.
- **Columns:**

| Column | Description |
|---|---|
| Invoice | Invoice number. 6 digits; a leading `C` marks a cancellation. Kept as string. |
| StockCode | Product code. Usually 5 digits plus an optional letter; special codes (POST, M, D, ...) are not products. Kept as string. |
| Description | Product name. |
| Quantity | Units per line. Negative for returns/cancellations. |
| InvoiceDate | Date and time of the invoice. |
| Price | Unit price in GBP. |
| Customer ID | 5-digit customer number. Nullable (`Int64`). |
| Country | Customer's country of residence. |
| source_sheet | Added on load: which Excel sheet the row came from. |

## How to Run

Windows, Python 3.12. From the project root:

```powershell
# 1. Create and activate the virtual environment
python -m venv venv
.\venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1

# 2. Install dependencies
pip install -r requirements.txt

# 3. Put the dataset at data\raw\online_retail_II.xlsx

# 4. Open the notebooks
jupyter notebook notebooks/
```

Run the notebooks in order: 01, then 02, then 03. The first run of notebook 01 reads the Excel file (slow, a few minutes) and caches it to `data/processed/online_retail_raw.parquet`. Later runs read the parquet in seconds. Delete the parquet to force a fresh read.

To run all notebooks headless and refresh the reports, figures and Power BI CSVs:

```powershell
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/01_data_loading_and_quality.ipynb
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/02_cleaning.ipynb
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace notebooks/03_analysis.ipynb
```

## Data Quality Notes

Full numbers are in [reports/data_quality.md](reports/data_quality.md) for the raw data and [reports/cleaning_log.md](reports/cleaning_log.md) for each cleaning step.

- **Overlapping sheets.** Both sheets contain 1 to 9 December 2010. The copy in the "Year 2010-2011" sheet is dropped.
- **Removed rows.** Exact duplicates, negative quantities that are not cancellations (stock adjustments), rows with Price <= 0, and bad-debt adjustments (invoices starting with "A"). 3.8% of raw rows are removed in total.
- **Kept and flagged.** Cancellations (`is_cancelled`, negative revenue), rows without a Customer ID (`has_customer`), non-product codes such as postage, fees and vouchers (`is_product`), December 2011 (`is_partial_month`, data ends on the 9th) and very large quantities (`is_outlier`).
- **Description is standardised for display only.** Each StockCode gets its most frequent description, so a product shows one consistent name in charts and tables. The original text is kept in `description_raw`. No analysis groups by description; products are always identified by StockCode.
- **Missing Customer ID.** About 15% of revenue has no Customer ID. It counts in revenue totals but not in cohort, RFM or LTV analysis.

## Analysis

Notebook `03_analysis.ipynb`, with the logic in `src/analysis.py`. Every analysis uses product rows only, so postage, fees, manual entries and vouchers are excluded. December 2011 is excluded from monthly trends and cohorts, but kept in all-time totals.

| Analysis | What it shows | Output |
|---|---|---|
| KPIs | Net and gross revenue, cancellation rate, orders, customers and AOV, all time and per year, plus a January-November like-for-like comparison | `reports/tables/kpis.csv` |
| Monthly trend | Net revenue per month, MoM growth, 2010 vs 2011 by calendar month | figures 01, 02 |
| Countries and products | Top 10 countries and products by net revenue, and a Pareto check | figures 03, 04, 05 |
| Cohort retention | Share of each first-purchase cohort buying again 1 to 23 months later | figure 06, `reports/tables/cohort_retention_matrix.csv` |
| RFM | Recency, frequency and monetary quintile scores mapped to 8 segments | figure 07 |
| LTV | Historical revenue per customer, overall and per segment, plus cumulative revenue after 3, 6 and 12 months | figure 08 |

**Definitions**
- **Net revenue** = sales + cancellations, where cancellations are negative.
- **Gross revenue** = sales only.
- **Orders** = non-cancelled invoices, excluding 270 orders that were fully cancelled later.
- **AOV** = revenue of those orders / orders.

## Key Findings

The like-for-like comparison covers January to November of 2010 and 2011. All figures are product revenue in GBP.

1. **Revenue is flat, and bigger orders offset fewer orders.** Net revenue grew **+2.3%**, from £8.38M to £8.57M. Orders fell **3.6%** and average order value rose **5.5%**, from £475 to £501. Growth has to come from order frequency and new customers.
2. **The business is highly seasonal.** September to November make up **41%** of January-November revenue. November peaks at **£1.43M**, about 3 times April. Stock and campaigns for the fourth quarter must be ready by August.
3. **Revenue is concentrated.** The top **20% of customers bring 77%** of customer revenue. **22% of products make 80%** of product revenue, and the **UK accounts for 85%**. **233 "Cannot Lose Them" customers** worth £895K have been inactive for about 11 months, which makes them the first win-back target.
4. **Most new customers do not come back.** Only about **21%** of a cohort buys again in month 1 and in month 3. A new customer brings **£634** in the first 3 months and **£1,373** in the first 12, so a second-purchase program has a large payoff.
5. **The 2011 cancellation spike is two bulk orders.** The rate rose from **2.5%** to **4.8%**, but two orders of 80,995 and 74,215 units, cancelled within minutes, account for about **£245K**. Without them the 2011 rate is **2.3%**. KPI reporting should flag one-off bulk orders.

## Limitations

- **Revenue, not profit.** The data has no cost or margin information, so every value figure is revenue. A high-revenue product or customer is not necessarily a profitable one.
- **Mostly wholesale customers.** Many customers are businesses buying in bulk. Order sizes, retention and LTV are not comparable with consumer e-commerce benchmarks.
- **UK-heavy.** The UK is 85% of revenue, so country-level results outside the UK rest on small samples.
- **December 2011 is partial.** The data ends on 9 December 2011. That month is excluded from trends and cohorts, so calendar-year totals for 2011 are not comparable with 2010.
- **Truncated history at the start.** December 2009 is the first month of data, so its "new customers" include long-standing customers. Retention and LTV are therefore also shown without that cohort.
- **Description is standardised for display.** Each StockCode shows its most frequent description, which hides some wording changes over time. All analysis uses StockCode.
- **Missing Customer ID.** About 15% of revenue has no Customer ID and is excluded from customer-level analysis.
- **Simple LTV.** LTV is a historical average, not a predictive model, and it ignores margin and discounting.
